## 1. Brand Foundation

**Studio Name:** Aura Tattoo & Meaning

**Location:** Portugal

**Owner:** Cathy da Costa Almeida

**In one sentence:**
*Aura is a tattoo studio that believes the mark on your skin should be as considered as the life that shaped it.*

**Tagline options:**
- *Wear your meaning.*
- *Where intention becomes permanent.*
- *A mark worth making.*

## 2. Brand Philosophy

### The Belief

A tattoo is one of the most intimate acts a person can undertake, an irreversible decision to carry something on their skin, forever. Most studios treat this as a transaction. Aura treats it as a ritual.

We are not chasing trends. We are not a volume studio. Aura exists for people who want to sit still with something, a memory, a loss, a chapter, a love, and have it translated into something lasting and beautiful. The tattoo is the final act. Everything before it matters just as much.

### The Soul of Aura

The brand draws from the principles of **wabi-sabi**, the Japanese philosophy of finding beauty in imperfection, transience, and the natural, but grounds these ideas in a distinctly **Portuguese** and **humanist** spirit. Not the aesthetics of the east, but the feeling: unhurried, soulful, deeply present.

Portugal has always understood this. There is *saudade*, the ache for something felt deeply, a bittersweet beauty in memory and longing. There is the slowness of the *tasca*, the worn tiles of Lisbon, the golden light. Aura draws from this. Warmth over minimalism. Soul over style.

The studio's atmosphere, service, and visual identity all ask the same question: *What does it feel like to be truly cared for?*

### What "Aura" Means

An aura is the invisible energy that surrounds a living thing. You can't see it, but you feel it. When you walk into Aura, you feel something. When you leave, you carry something more. The brand exists in that feeling, the intangible that becomes permanent.

## 3. Positioning

### Audience

Aura speaks to thoughtful adults, people who have been thinking about their tattoo for months or years. They are not impulsive. They are likely already drawn to considered aesthetics: slow living, intentional spaces, quality over quantity. They may be:

- First-time tattoo clients who want to be certain
- Experienced collectors seeking a different kind of studio experience
- People marking a significant life moment (grief, transition, celebration, survival)
- Design-literate individuals who appreciate restraint and craft

**Psychographic:** They meditate or wish they did. They own things that last. They read, feel deeply, and are slightly wary of anything that feels performative or loud.

### Market Position

Aura occupies the space between **boutique art studio** and **wellness experience**, a category that currently does not exist in the Portuguese tattoo market. This is the opportunity.

| | Volume Studios | Traditional Boutique | **Aura** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experience | Transactional | Art-focused | Ritual + Art |
| Atmosphere | Busy/loud | Professional | Calm/intentional |
| Client journey | Booking → tattoo | Consultation → tattoo | Full sensory journey |
| Aftercare | Basic leaflet | Good practice | Branded care ritual |
| Emotional tone | Neutral | Respectful | Deeply human |

### Brand Promise

*Every person who walks into Aura leaves carrying something more considered, more personal, and more permanent than they expected, both on their skin and in how they feel.*

## 4. Voice & Tone

### The Voice

Aura speaks the way a thoughtful friend might, one who listens more than they speak, chooses words carefully, and never rushes. The voice is warm but not casual, poetic but not pretentious, confident but never loud.

**Aura speaks in:**
- Short, considered sentences
- Present tense where possible (it feels immediate, alive)
- Plain language elevated by one well-chosen word
- Questions that invite reflection

**Aura does not speak in:**
- Tattoo industry clichés ("ink," "fresh," "badass," "sleeved up")
- Hyperbole or urgency ("last spots," "limited time")
- Spiritual jargon disconnected from the person (no chakras, no "vibrations")
- Corporate or formal register

### Tone Across Contexts

**Website copy:** Still, unhurried. Like reading something carved in stone, purposeful, permanent.

**Instagram captions:** Intimate and brief. One observation, one feeling, one image. No hashtag spam.

**Booking confirmation:** Warm and reassuring. The client has made a decision. Honour that.

**Consultation follow-up:** Deeply personal. Reference what they shared. Make them feel heard.

**Aftercare messaging:** Caring and practical. Like a letter from someone who genuinely wants the work to heal beautifully.

### Writing Examples

**Do:** *Your tattoo is still becoming. Here's how to care for it while it settles.*

**Don't:** *🔥 Fresh ink care tips!! Keep it moisturised and out of the sun 🌊*

**Do:** *We'd love to hear the story behind this one, if you'd like to share it.*

**Don't:** *Tell us your tattoo inspo! What made you choose this design?*

## 5. Visual Identity

### The Feeling

Before describing any specific colour or typeface, the visual identity of Aura should always communicate:

- **Stillness**, nothing feels rushed, crowded, or competing
- **Warmth**, not cold minimalism; there is humanity here
- **Intention**, every element was chosen, nothing is default
- **Imperfect beauty**, wabi-sabi applied visually: texture over perfection, organic over geometric

### Overall Aesthetic Direction

Aura sits visually in the space between a high-end **Portuguese design studio** and a **natural apothecary**. Think: linen paper, natural light, a warm stone surface, a single bloom in a handmade ceramic. Nothing disposable. Nothing over-designed.

Inspiration can be drawn from: Portuguese tile craftsmanship, the soft terracotta and cream of Lisbon's architecture, the textures of natural materials, cork, linen, unpolished stone, and the restrained elegance of Scandinavian and Japanese design where they overlap.

## 6. Typography

### Primary Typeface, Modern Serif

The primary typeface for Aura should be a **modern serif**, elegant, with visible contrast between thick and thin strokes, but contemporary rather than classical. The letters should feel both timeless and designed.

**Recommended:** Cormorant Garamond, Playfair Display, or EB Garamond
*(Note for designer: commission or license a primary serif that feels distinctly Aura's, slightly literary, unhurried, considered)*

Use for: Logo, headings, pull quotes, campaign statements

### Secondary Typeface, Clean Sans-Serif

A single, quiet sans-serif to handle body text, labels, and functional copy. It should disappear into the background, readable and neutral.

**Recommended:** Inter, Neue Haas Grotesk, or DM Sans

Use for: Body copy, captions, form labels, small UI elements

### Type Rules

- Headlines are set in **Title Case** or **lowercase**, never all caps (too loud for Aura's register)
- Generous line-height (1.6–1.8 for body, 1.2–1.3 for display)
- Tracking: slightly open for headings, normal for body
- Never more than two typefaces active in any single piece of communication
- Type colour should always come from the palette, no pure black, always warm dark

## 7. Colour Palette

### Philosophy

Aura's palette is built on restraint. The neutrals do the heavy lifting, warm, earthy, deeply liveable tones that feel like the walls of a very considered home. The accent is singular. It is used sparingly, which is what gives it power.

### Neutral Foundation

| Name | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Cartão** *(Card)* | Warm off-white, the colour of uncoated cotton paper | Primary background, negative space |
| **Areia** *(Sand)* | Soft warm sand, slightly golden | Secondary surfaces, paper, packaging |
| **Pedra** *(Stone)* | Warm mid-grey with an undertone of taupe | Dividers, subtle UI elements, texture |
| **Terra** *(Earth)* | Deep warm charcoal-brown, almost black | Primary text, logo |

*Note: All neutrals lean warm (yellow/red undertone). Cool greys are not part of the Aura palette.*

### Accent Tone

| Name | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Barro** *(Clay)* | A single muted terracotta-clay, dusty, warm, not orange | Accent elements, CTA buttons, logo mark, key moments |

*Barro* is Aura's heartbeat. It carries Portuguese earthiness, the terracotta of the rooftops, the warmth of a hand-thrown bowl, the colour of something made by hand. It should never be used as a background for large areas. It punctuates. It draws the eye. It is the warmth in an otherwise quiet room.

**Do:** Use Barro on one element per layout, a button, a line, a small icon, a word in a headline.

**Don't:** Fill a section with Barro. Don't combine it with competing warm tones.

### Palette Summary

```
Cartão    #F5F0EB  , near-white, warm paper
Areia     #E8DDD0  , sand, warm off-white
Pedra     #AFA49A  , stone mid-tone
Terra     #2C2420  , deep warm dark
Barro     #B56F52  , terracotta clay accent
```

*(Final hex values to be confirmed with designer during brand build, the above are directional.)*

## 8. The Aura Experience, Moments

The Aura experience is a sequence of **intentional moments**, small rituals that accumulate into something unforgettable. Each moment is an opportunity to express the brand. Each moment says: *you are not just a client, you are a person, and this matters.*

Below are the key experience moments and how Aura inhabits each one.

---

### Moment 1, The Discovery

*The first contact with the brand, usually digital.*

The website, Instagram, and any word-of-mouth referral should give the same feeling: a deep breath. The pacing of the website is slow. There is no "Book Now" button blinking in the hero. There is a story first. Then an invitation.

**Brand expression:** Photography of the studio space (not just tattoos), a brief manifesto in elegant type, the booking process framed as a "conversation request" rather than a transaction.

---

### Moment 2, The Consultation Request

*The client reaches out.*

The booking form (or contact form) is brief, unhurried, and asks one important question beyond the practical: *"Is there a story behind what you'd like to create?"* This single question sets the tone for everything that follows.

**Brand expression:** Calm form design, warm confirmation email, a short message that acknowledges their story with care. Response within 24–48 hours, written personally, not automated.

---

### Moment 3, The Welcome

*The client arrives at the studio.*

The first moment inside Aura should be sensory. Music is ambient, acoustic, barely audible, nothing with lyrics. The scent is faint and natural, eucalyptus, cedar, or a custom room spray developed for the studio. The space is clean, warm, and still.

**The Tea Ritual:** Every client who arrives is offered a warm drink, a selection of Portuguese herbal teas (tila, camomila, hortelã) or still water with lemon and mint. The offering is made without fuss. A small tray, a handwritten card listing the options, time to sit and settle.

This is not a gimmick. This is the pace of Aura. *We are not rushing. You have time. Breathe.*

**Brand expression:** Branded tea tray (simple, ceramic or natural material), printed menu card in Aura's typography, soft lighting, no clinical overhead fluorescents.

---

### Moment 4, The Meaning Conversation

*The consultation before the work begins.*

Aura's artists are trained not just in craft, but in listening. The consultation is not about which design file to pull up. It begins with: *"Tell me about this."* The artist sits across from the client, not behind a desk, and listens.

This conversation is not recorded. It is not filed. It is simply heard. From it, the design is informed or refined.

**Brand expression:** A simple wooden clipboard with high-quality paper for sketching together. The artist sketches in front of the client when adjustments are made, a live, human, non-digital process where possible. Nothing feels clinical.

---

### Moment 5, The Setup

*Preparation before the session begins.*

As the artist prepares their station, the client is settled. This is a liminal moment, the waiting-before. Aura treats it with care.

A curated playlist is offered (the client can choose a mood: *quiet / present / peaceful*). If the session is long, reading material is available, not magazines, but small curated books: poetry, travel, philosophy, things consistent with the Aura world.

**Brand expression:** A small printed card listing the playlist moods. Books from Aura's curated selection, left without comment. A glass of water, refreshed. The station is prepared with visible care, each tool laid out deliberately.

---

### Moment 6, The Session

*The work itself.*

This is the artist's domain. Aura does not interfere with the creative act. But the environment is managed:

- Sound: ambient, never intrusive
- Light: warm task lighting, not clinical white
- Check-ins: the artist checks on the client's comfort naturally, without interrupting flow
- Breaks: offered gently for longer sessions, *"Shall we pause a moment?"*

The client is never made to feel like they're on a production line. If they need to talk, the artist is present. If they need silence, that is offered too.

---

### Moment 7, The Reveal

*The tattoo is finished and wrapped.*

This moment deserves ceremony. The mirror is offered properly. There is a pause, not immediately asking "what do you think?" but allowing the client to sit with it for a moment.

The artist says very little. The work speaks.

When the client is ready, Aura's care pack is presented.

---

### Moment 8, The Aftercare Pack

*The client leaves with something.*

The Aura Aftercare Pack is a small, considered gift, not a branded plastic bag with a leaflet. It is the physical extension of the studio experience into the client's home.

**The pack contains:**

- **A branded moisturiser**, a small-batch, fragrance-free healing moisturiser in a minimal amber glass jar with Aura's label. Clean ingredients, sourced where possible from Portuguese producers. Named something simple: *Cura* (Healing).
- **A healing instruction card**, not a standard photocopy. A beautifully printed card in Aura's type, with the aftercare instructions written in Aura's voice: calm, caring, precise.
- **A small organic cotton cloth**, for gentle cleaning during healing. Minimal branding.
- **A note from the artist**, a brief handwritten line. Not a template. *"Thank you for trusting me with this."*

The pack is wrapped simply, natural tissue, a wax seal or a simple ribbon in Barro/terracotta. It is something the client will photograph. It is something they will keep.

**Brand expression:** The outer packaging is kraft or natural linen. The label design uses Aura's full typography and palette. The pack feels like something from a considered apothecary, not a tattoo parlour.

---

### Moment 9, The Healing Check-In

*3–5 days after the session.*

A brief, personal message from the studio (or the artist directly) checking on the healing. Not automated. Not a newsletter. A short, genuine note.

*"Hope the first few days are going smoothly. The itching is normal, resist! If anything looks unusual, don't hesitate to reach out."*

This builds loyalty. It communicates that the relationship didn't end when the client walked out.

---

### Moment 10, The Full Reveal

*4–6 weeks later, once the tattoo has fully settled.*

Aura invites clients to share a photo of the healed tattoo, not publicly unless they wish to, but as a closing of the loop. The artist sees it. The studio knows it settled beautifully.

If the client is happy to share publicly, this content becomes some of Aura's best marketing, not fresh-ink photos (too common), but *healed* tattoos on real skin, with real stories.

**Brand expression:** A gentle follow-up message, a discount or priority booking for their next session as a gesture of appreciation.

## 10. Photography & Visual Direction

### Hero Images

Photography for Aura should feel like it was captured in natural light, by someone who waited for the right moment. No harsh flash, no overexposed studio shots.

**Subjects:**
- Healed tattoos on natural skin tones, photographed as you would a painting, not a product
- The studio space, corners, surfaces, objects, never a full-room shot that feels like real estate
- Hands, the artist's hands, the client's hands, the moment of connection
- Objects, the tea tray, the aftercare pack, a book left open, the pen on the clipboard
- People, faces when the moment is right; never forced, never posed artificially

**What to avoid:**
- Bright, saturated tattoo close-ups on a blank background
- Clichéd "tattoo studio" imagery (skulls, flash sheets on walls, gloves-and-needle shots)
- Overly stylised flat lays
- Stock photography of any kind

### Filter & Editing Direction

- Warm, slightly desaturated
- Lifted shadows (nothing too dark or contrasty)
- Skin tones preserved, not over-processed
- Shot on film or edited to feel like it

## 11. Social Media Guidelines

### Instagram

Aura's Instagram is a window into the studio's world, not a portfolio feed and not a lifestyle brand. It is somewhere in between.

**Content pillars:**

1. **The Work**, healed tattoos, with the story (as much as the client wishes to share). Brief caption. Let the image breathe.
2. **The Studio**, the space, the objects, the light at different times of day. Seasonal moments.
3. **The Meaning**, short reflections. A quote from a client (anonymised and with permission). A thought from the artist. Never preachy.
4. **The Process**, sketches, consultation moments (never the client's face without permission), the artist's hand at work.
5. **The Pack / Aftercare**, the products and rituals, photographed beautifully.

**Cadence:** Quality over frequency. 3–4 posts per week maximum. Stories can be more regular and candid.

**Hashtag philosophy:** Use 3–5 considered hashtags maximum. No hashtag walls. Never hashtags that would attract the wrong audience (e.g. no #tattoocommunity if it brings volume-studio energy).

**Comments:** Respond to every meaningful comment. Briefly, warmly. In the brand's voice.

## 12. What Aura Is Not

To protect the integrity of the brand, it is as important to define what Aura does not do as what it does.

Aura is **not** a flash studio. Walk-ins are not the model. Every client is expected, every session is prepared.

Aura is **not** trend-driven. No "most requested styles." Aura does not chase what is popular on social media this season.

Aura is **not** a wellness brand performing spirituality. There are no crystals on the reception desk. No mentions of "energy cleansing." The soul of Aura is humanist, it is about people, stories, and meaning. Not belief systems.

Aura is **not** Asian-themed. The wabi-sabi and japandi philosophical influences are *felt*, not displayed. There are no cherry blossoms on the walls, no kanji in the branding, no cultural appropriation of Japanese aesthetics. The feeling is inspired; the execution is Portuguese and European.

Aura is **not** loud. The brand never shouts. It speaks quietly and trusts that the right people will hear it.
